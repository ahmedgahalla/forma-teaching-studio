# Phase 3 — demo path proposal

**Status:** proposed; implementation not started · **Branch:** `ahmed/phase-3-demo-proposal` · **PR:** pending · **Owner:** builder · **Date:** 2026-09-26

## Decision requested

Approve a focused, model-first lecture demo lasting **3 minutes 40 seconds**, with two featured setups: **Movement types** (translation versus tipping) and **Anchorage and space use**. Make a **conceptual translation pressure–tension illustration** the central teaching moment. Keep **Midline diastema** as the visually simpler substitute for anchorage, chosen before the presentation rather than added to the timed script.

This is a proposal for review, not an approved roadmap or a description of shipped features. This PR changes documentation only. The user and auditor choose the cases, biology depth and delivery order before implementation begins. The original three-case pitch remains a viable higher-cost alternative, compared below.

The audience is an orthodontist who lectures at universities, watching in person and in a recorded video. Presentable beats feature-complete: one coherent workspace, clear questions before answers, large visible anatomy, deterministic replay and a reliable return to the professor's setup. Try Mode stays available for optional exploration; AI and voice are not dependencies of the rehearsed route.

## Biology: teach the mechanism without claiming a simulation

**First delivery:** a clear, labelled **conceptual section** beside a paused, focused 3D tooth. Use existing root/cutaway anatomy for orientation, but draw an authored 2D/SVG tooth–ligament–bone schematic with exaggerated ligament thickness. The inset is not a stress heatmap registered to the current mesh. This avoids making the existing fixed socket appear to calculate tissue response.

Three reversible stages, driven by the same active playback strip:

1. **Observe:** identify root, periodontal ligament and alveolar bone; show a direction arrow and ask which region compresses.
2. **Reveal zones:** compressed-side ligament narrows, opposite-side ligament stretches in an idealized translation schematic. Use amber hatching plus “Compression” and blue dots plus “Tension”; labels and patterns carry meaning without colour alone. No MPa, force magnitude, biological speed or patient-specific numbers.
3. **Explain remodelling:** show a small scalloped change at the compression-side bone surface labelled “Osteoclast-associated resorption” and new layers at the tension-side bone surface labelled “Osteoblast-associated formation”. Animate an explanatory sequence, with the explicit caption “Conceptual sequence”; do not imply the cellular processes happen instantly, exclusively, or in a universal one-after-the-other order.

Pressure/tension is a useful introductory account, not a complete model of tooth movement. The supporting-tissue response depends on the loading and biological context. The source panel can explain the simplification without covering the lecture screen with disclaimers. An orthodontic educator should approve the zones, arrows and narration before this is presented as teaching material; no such review has occurred yet.

**Case mapping:** translation on incisor 11 supports the first schematic, and a later isolated canine translation can reuse it with matching direction labels. Anchorage uses the same concept only as a selected representative tooth, not as a map of all forces. Diastema needs no added biology segment. For a tipping biology extension, choose a separate authored uncontrolled-tipping example with an illustrative pivot within the supported root region and corresponding opposite cervical/apical zones; other pivot conditions need different diagrams; it cannot inherit a simple left/right translation colouring. Premolar derotation requires a separate circumferential-sector explanation. Molar uprighting adds multiple roots/furcation and needs its own reviewed illustration. Those extensions are optional scope decisions, not automatic consequences of showing a rotated mesh.

**Implementation boundary for a later PR:** store stages, captions, labels and normalized illustration poses as content data. Extend validated presentation actions only for missing bounded stage/layer controls; keep tooth transforms through the existing teaching runtime. Pause and retain case progress when the tissue step owns playback; returning restores the exact case frame and camera. Scrubbing/restart must reproduce the same illustration. Allocate graphics outside the render loop and dispose resources. Do not change solver outputs or claim an inferred physiological pivot.

### Evidence to ground the biology content

The biological sources below ground terminology and constrain the planned illustration; they do not validate Forma's synthetic paths or establish patient treatment timing. The final graphic is an authored teaching interpretation, not a reproduction of a paper's results.

- [Wu et al., 2018 — canine finite-element study](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6052357/): reports different ligament patterns for translation, tipping and axial rotation. It informs the distinction between diagrams, not a numerical force threshold or Forma stress map.
- [Yang et al., 2018 — RANKL deletion experiment](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/29483595/): reducing this signalling pathway in periodontal-ligament/bone-lining cells reduced osteoclast formation and tooth movement in mice. This supports a cell-mediated explanation rather than teeth simply cutting through bone.
- [Kamei et al., 2022 — Semaphorin 3A experiments](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41598-022-13217-x.pdf): mouse and cell experiments examine regulation of formation and resorption on tension/compression sides. These support the cell labels while showing biology involves more than a two-colour rule.
- [Rizk et al., 2023 — 3D periodontal remodelling study](https://cris.maastrichtuniversity.nl/en/publications/periodontal-ligament-and-alveolar-bone-remodeling-during-long-ort/): a mouse study found spatially nonuniform changes. Use this as a reason to avoid universal zone boundaries or extrapolated human timing.
