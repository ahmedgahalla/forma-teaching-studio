## Request and outcome

The owner requested proper demo lectures, then asked to use Claude's model and preferred UI. The local source was located in Claude's `scratch-2026-09-26-716563` output. This later request supersedes the temporary lecture-content deferral in 3.20. No lecture creator is restored; professor-supplied lectures and cases remain later work.

Before, Forma used its earlier 28-tooth asset, cooler studio controls and one finished lecture. This integration uses Claude's enamel/root/gingiva surfaces and material treatment, adapts the warm instrument-panel interface to the existing runtime, and offers three ready-made lectures.

## Model and rendering

- The producer's unmodified raw GLB (19,205,456 bytes) and metadata are bundled as `forma-atlas-v1`. Their verified hashes, generation time and coordinate mapping are recorded in [snapshot provenance](../../../assets/anatomy/claude-atlas/README.md). Claude's source and ongoing rebuild were not modified.
- The source contains 32 teeth. The adapter explicitly uses the existing 28 supported teaching IDs; wisdom teeth remain in the preserved export but are not displayed/selectable. This keeps authored cases, lecture validation and existing anatomy content consistent.
- Separate enamel and cementum primitives share actual crown-centre pivots. Anatomical directions come from metadata; bracket locations are measured on the actual buccal crown surface. The already fitted atlas gets no legacy 3 mm arch-closing correction.
- Exported smooth normals, albedo, normalized near/far AO, thickness, tissue-zone data and UVs survive display and saved-case round trips. Tagged cases missing required material channels are rejected. Legacy asset fixtures and saved cases remain supported.
- Source physical shaders, wrapped/scattered lighting, microtextures, gum-zone response, warm backdrop and camera-relative lights are adapted for the installed Three r186. Shader callbacks survive status/focus clones; generated textures have explicit owners. The stipple raster uses deterministic texel sampling rather than Canvas antialiasing, so pixel-identical reproduction is not claimed. The existing translucent gum controls remain.
- The geometry audit was regenerated from the new runtime asset: the reference has no detected crown/root surface intersections. Authored-path intersections remain recorded and shown as known limitations; the previous asset's results were not reused.
- All 17 mechanics catalog variants are exercised with the actual atlas GLB: valid tooth/anchor references, finite solver output, deterministic replay and unchanged starting configuration. This verifies software compatibility, not clinical accuracy.

## UI and teaching behavior

- Warm near-black panels, enamel-colored text, restrained blue selection, Instrument Serif titles and IBM Plex controls/data follow the selected Claude design. Fonts and SIL licenses are local assets, with an offline build check.
- A direct Front / Left / Right / Upper / Lower / ¾ camera rail, compact Display disclosure, odontogram and selected-tooth inspector connect to Forma's model. Frame, isolate and study controls retain their actual capabilities. Selecting a hidden-arch tooth reveals it; additive cross-arch selection shows both arches.
- The inspector has its own region beside the canvas on desktop, preserving the model's actual fit area rather than covering its geometry. Narrow screens retain chart/selection controls and existing study access. Existing Explore/Lecture, advanced tools, commands, voice, playback, audience projection and return paths remain.
- The atlas has physical root surfaces but no compatible branch-wise bone/PDL profiles. Supporting sleeves and cutaway commands are explicitly unavailable on atlas scenes; no old profiles are grafted onto it. The legacy socket classroom uses its own clearly described schematic model, while appliance classrooms use the atlas. The biology lecture and bone/ligament glossary entries use separate local tissue diagrams. Workflow-to-Explore transfer preserves the asset identity and validates the schematic lesson against its own canonical base.

## Demo lectures

1. **Translation and tipping** — the existing four-step sample and stable identifier are preserved. Predicted movement, translation, tipping and recap; suggested 3–4 minutes.
2. **Space closure and anchorage** — six steps compare the same-start held-posterior and shared-space authored paths, pause at endpoints, then ask an exit question. Notes distinguish chosen movement allocation from calculated force equilibrium.
3. **Why teeth move** — six steps connect the root/ligament/bone relationship to compression-associated resorption and tension-associated formation, explain the limits of fixed two-sided diagrams, and distinguish visible movement from biological prediction.

Each new lecture has presenter notes, suggested pacing, student questions, answer reveals, objectives and primary-source links. A compact Demo lectures disclosure selects through validated presentation actions; a repeated Lecture click preserves the active lecture/step. Comparison controls only offer demonstrations present in the selected lecture. Strict optional `biology` step data selects the corresponding vignette and resets between appropriate steps. No authoring, import or lecture storage is introduced.
